### Overview
The **hydroxyl radical (•OH)** produced when **ROS interacts with Fe²⁺** in the **Fenton reaction** is the most damaging oxidative species known in biology.  
Unlike superoxide or hydrogen peroxide, which can be neutralized by enzymes like **SOD** or **catalase**, the hydroxyl radical reacts instantly and indiscriminately with any nearby molecule — lipids, DNA, or proteins — leaving no opportunity for repair.  
This is what makes iron-catalyzed ROS uniquely catastrophic: it transforms oxidative stress from a **signaling imbalance** into a **chemical chain reaction of destruction**.  
When stress drives iron release and overwhelms antioxidants, this radical chemistry triggers **ferroptosis**, a self-amplifying collapse of cellular integrity.

---

### Why Iron Is Required
- **Fe²⁺ ↔ Fe³⁺ cycling** enables continuous hydroxyl radical production.  
- **•OH radicals** attack polyunsaturated fatty acids, driving self-propagating lipid peroxidation.  
- Other metals cannot replicate this catalytic efficiency under biological conditions.  
- Without iron, oxidative stress may still occur but lacks the chain reaction that defines ferroptosis.

---

### How Stress Contributes to Iron Dysregulation
1. **Cortisol–HO-1 Pathway:** Chronic stress elevates cortisol, which upregulates **heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)** via **Bach1–HO-1 signaling**, releasing Fe²⁺ from heme.  
2. **Mitochondrial Breakdown:** Prolonged ROS exposure damages iron–sulfur cluster proteins, releasing iron into the cytoplasm.  
3. **Glutathione and GPX4 Collapse:** Stress depletes glutathione, disabling **GPX4**, the enzyme that neutralizes lipid peroxides.  
4. **Inflammatory Retention:** IL-6–driven **hepcidin** expression traps iron inside cells, increasing local Fe²⁺ concentration and oxidative potential.

---

### Sequence
1. **Chronic ROS** → H₂O₂ accumulation  
2. **H₂O₂ + Fe²⁺ (Fenton Reaction)** → •OH  
3. **•OH attacks lipids** → peroxidation chain reaction  
4. **GPX4 depletion** → loss of antioxidant control  
5. **Membrane rupture** → **ferroptotic death**

---

### Summary
Under chronic stress, the **apoptotic ROS pathway** (cytochrome c release) can escalate into **ferroptosis** when iron metabolism destabilizes.  
Stress effectively **converts regulated oxidative death into uncontrolled chemical combustion**, driven by hydroxyl radicals that destroy everything they touch.  
Apoptosis is surgical. Ferroptosis is systemic. Both begin with **ROS**, but iron decides the outcome — and the presence of iron ensures the most devastating form of oxidative injury.
