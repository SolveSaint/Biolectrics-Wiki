### Overview
The **hydroxyl radical (•OH)** formed when **ROS interacts with Fe²⁺** through the **Fenton reaction** is the most damaging oxidative species in biology.  
Unlike superoxide or hydrogen peroxide, which are neutralized by **SOD** or **catalase**, hydroxyl radicals react immediately with lipids, proteins, and DNA.  
Iron-catalyzed ROS transforms oxidative stress from a reversible imbalance into a **chain reaction of lipid peroxidation** that destroys membranes and mitochondria.  
When stress increases free iron and depletes antioxidants, this chemistry triggers **ferroptosis**, the most destructive form of oxidative cell death [(Liu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.15.682717).

---

### Why Iron Is Required
**Fe²⁺ ↔ Fe³⁺ cycling** sustains continuous hydroxyl radical production.  
Hydroxyl radicals oxidize polyunsaturated fatty acids, driving **self-propagating lipid peroxidation** across membranes.  
Other metals cannot catalyze this process under physiological conditions.  
Without iron, ROS can still form but cannot sustain the runaway lipid oxidation that defines ferroptosis.

---

### How Stress Contributes to Iron Dysregulation
1. **Cortisol–HO-1 Pathway:** Chronic stress activates **HO-1** through **Bach1–HO-1 signaling**, releasing Fe²⁺ from heme and enlarging the labile iron pool [(Liu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.15.682717).  
2. **Mitochondrial Breakdown:** Sustained ROS damages **iron–sulfur cluster proteins**, further releasing Fe²⁺ into mitochondria and cytoplasm.  
3. **PGRMC1 Dysfunction:** **Progesterone receptor membrane component 1 (PGRMC1)** regulates mitochondrial heme export through **mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs)**.  
   - PGRMC1 loss or stress-related dysfunction causes **heme accumulation** in mitochondria.  
   - Excess heme releases Fe²⁺, enhancing **Fenton chemistry** and **ferroptotic vulnerability** [(Piel et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinorgbio.2025.113093).  
1. **Glutathione and GPX4 Collapse:** Chronic stress depletes **glutathione**, disabling **GPX4**, the enzyme that detoxifies lipid peroxides.  
2. **Inflammatory Retention:** **IL-6–driven hepcidin** expression traps iron inside neurons and glia, raising intracellular Fe²⁺ and redox potential.

---

### Sequence
1. **Mitochondrial ROS leak** → superoxide (O₂•⁻) → H₂O₂ via SOD conversion.  
2. **H₂O₂ + Fe²⁺ (Fenton reaction)** → hydroxyl radicals (•OH).  
3. **•OH** oxidizes polyunsaturated lipids → lipid peroxidation chain reaction.  
4. **GPX4 depletion** removes antioxidant control.  
5. **Membrane rupture** and mitochondrial collapse → **ferroptotic death**.

---

### Summary
Under chronic stress, persistent ROS production, **HO-1 activation**, and **PGRMC1 dysfunction** destabilize mitochondrial heme and iron balance.  
Hydrogen peroxide generated from **ETC superoxide leak** reacts with Fe²⁺ to form hydroxyl radicals that drive unrestrained lipid peroxidation.  
This converts regulated oxidative signaling into **irreversible ferroptotic damage**.  
Apoptosis is structured and recoverable; ferroptosis is **chemical and terminal**, marking the endpoint of chronic stress-induced neurodegeneration.
