### Summary
Chronic stress drives the same oxidative collapse seen in cardiovascular and neurodegenerative disease. Excess cortisol and glutamate signaling elevate mitochondrial ROS, activating **Bach1–HO-1** and destabilizing iron homeostasis.  
Two main sources of Fe²⁺ initiate the Fenton reaction: oxidative damage to **iron–sulfur cluster proteins** and heme accumulation caused by **PGRMC1 dysfunction** at mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs).  
The resulting interaction of hydrogen peroxide with Fe²⁺ produces hydroxyl radicals that trigger **lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis**. Stress converts normal oxidative signaling into a self-sustaining chemical chain reaction that destroys neurons and glia.

---

### Mechanistic Sequence

1. **Cortisol–HO-1 signaling and iron release**  
   Chronic stress activates **HO-1** through **Bach1–HO-1** derepression, releasing Fe²⁺ from heme and enlarging the labile iron pool [(Liu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2025.104024).

2. **Mitochondrial ROS production and Fe–S cluster oxidation**  
   Superoxide generated by the electron transport chain converts to H₂O₂ via SOD. Continuous ROS oxidizes **iron–sulfur clusters** in mitochondrial enzymes, releasing Fe²⁺ into both mitochondria and cytoplasm, increasing Fenton reactivity.

3. **PGRMC1 dysfunction and heme accumulation**  
   **PGRMC1** controls heme export from mitochondria through **MAM redox coupling**. Loss or stress-induced inhibition of PGRMC1 blocks heme trafficking, causing mitochondrial heme buildup. Oxidative cleavage of this heme releases Fe²⁺, enhancing **Fenton chemistry** and **ferroptotic sensitivity** [(Piel et al., 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-01314-3).

4. **Fenton reaction and hydroxyl radical generation**  
   Hydrogen peroxide reacts with Fe²⁺ to form **hydroxyl radicals (•OH)**. These radicals are the most reactive form of ROS and initiate chain **lipid peroxidation**, damaging mitochondrial and plasma membranes.

5. **GPX4 depletion and antioxidant loss**  
   Glutathione depletion disables **GPX4**, preventing detoxification of lipid peroxides and allowing peroxidation to spread through membranes.

6. **Inflammatory iron retention**  
   **IL-6–driven hepcidin** signaling restricts iron export, trapping Fe²⁺ inside neurons and glia, maintaining the redox imbalance.

7. **Membrane failure and ferroptosis**  
   Progressive peroxidation causes mitochondrial collapse, lipid fragmentation, and cell rupture — the defining features of ferroptotic death.

---

### Mechanistic Overview
- **Fe–S cluster oxidation** and **heme accumulation** represent two convergent iron sources under chronic stress.  
- **PGRMC1** functions as a redox gatekeeper at MAMs; its failure converts controlled oxidative metabolism into unregulated ferroptotic chemistry.  
- **Hydrogen peroxide** from mitochondrial ROS initiates the process, while **Fe²⁺** from heme and cluster proteins sustains it.  
- **HO-1 activation**, **GPX4 depletion**, and **hepcidin retention** lock the system into a self-amplifying oxidative loop that drives neurodegeneration.

---

### Interpretation
Stress-induced iron dysregulation explains why ferroptosis appears as the endpoint in many idiopathic neurological diseases. The combination of **Bach1–HO-1 activation**, **Fe–S destabilization**, and **PGRMC1 loss** creates a redox-positive feedback loop that overwhelms cellular repair.  
Apoptosis marks early damage; ferroptosis marks the irreversible transition to terminal degeneration.
