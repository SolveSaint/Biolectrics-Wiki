### Summary
Cardiovascular disease is the global leading cause of death and expresses the same oxidative terminal mechanism observed in neurodegenerative disease. The convergence of iron overload and reactive oxygen species results in lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death within cardiomyocytes and vascular endothelium. This process mirrors the stress, glutamate, ROS cascade seen in ALS and Alzheimer’s disease, which establishes a unified model of excitotoxicity driven degeneration across brain and heart.

### Mechanistic Sequence

1. **Iron dysregulation and Fenton chemistry**  
   Excess Fe²⁺ reacts with H₂O₂ to generate hydroxyl radicals through the Fenton reaction, which is the most destructive form of ROS. These radicals attack lipids, proteins, and mitochondrial DNA, which drives peroxidation and structural collapse ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)). HO-1 and TFR1 are upregulated in cardiac tissue during ischemia and heart failure, which confirms chronic iron mobilization and overload ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).

2. **Mitochondrial ROS production**  
   Mitochria act as both source and target of oxidative damage. During ischemia and reperfusion, calcium influx increases oxidative phosphorylation throughput, which increases electron leakage through complexes I and III and produces hydrogen peroxide ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)). When H₂O₂ reacts with Fe²⁺, it yields hydroxyl radicals that damage mitochondrial membranes and DNA ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)).

3. **GPX4 depletion and GSH loss**  
   GPX4 normally reduces lipid hydroperoxides and prevents membrane oxidation. Stress, hypoxia, and sustained ROS generation deplete GPX4 and glutathione, which leaves polyunsaturated lipids vulnerable ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)). Ferroptotic injury is defined by this antioxidant collapse and produces uncontrolled lipid peroxidation ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).

4. **Lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic rupture**  
   The oxidation of membrane phospholipids generates malondialdehyde and 4-hydroxynonenal, which are hallmarks of ferroptosis ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)). Elevated ACSL4 facilitates synthesis of oxidizable lipids that amplify peroxidation and culminate in outer membrane rupture and ferroptotic death ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).

5. **Inflammation and endothelial activation**  
   Oxidized phospholipids activate NF-κB and toll-like receptor pathways, which increase cytokine release and adhesion molecule expression ([Sugamura & Keaney, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2011.05.004)). This chronic inflammatory feedback sustains endothelial dysfunction and plaque formation, which is a key step in atherosclerosis ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).

6. **Ferroptotic cell death and tissue failure**  
   Iron driven lipid peroxidation ruptures cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells and releases DAMPs that propagate inflammatory remodeling ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)). The result is tissue necrosis, fibrotic scarring, and progressive heart failure, which follows the same molecular logic as excitotoxic neuronal death ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)).

### Parallel to Neurodegeneration

| Mechanistic node | Cardiovascular expression | Neural analog |
|---|---|---|
| Iron overload with HO-1 and TFR1 | Plaques, infarct rim, ischemic myocardium | ALS, AD, PD with cortical and nigral deposits |
| GPX4 depletion with GSH loss | Heart failure and IRI myocardium | Motor cortex and hippocampus |
| Lipid peroxidation with 4-HNE | Atherosclerotic and infarct tissue | Amyloid and Lewy body regions |
| Mitochondrial ROS | ETC leak with Ca²⁺ overload in myocardium | Complex I and III dysfunction |
| Ferroptosis inhibition with chelators or Fer-1 | Cardioprotection ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)) | Neuronal protection with the same mechanism |

### Therapeutic Insights
- Iron chelation limits hydroxyl radical formation and reduces reperfusion injury ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)).  
- Ferroptosis inhibitors such as ferrostatin-1 and liproxstatin-1 preserve myocardial tissue ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).  
- NRF2 activation restores redox control and mitigates cardiomyocyte death ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).  
- Mitochondria targeted antioxidants such as SS-31 and MitoQ lower ROS generation ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)).  
- Stress modulation reduces chronic excitatory drive and prevents sustained mitochondrial overload and iron release.

### Interpretation
CVD and neurodegeneration lie along the same oxidative continuum. Chronic stress elevates cortisol and excitatory signaling, which increases calcium flux and mitochondrial throughput. The resulting ROS surplus, when combined with labile iron, generates hydroxyl radicals that catalyze ferroptosis. This stress, iron, ROS triad defines the endpoint of biological overdrive. Neuronal loss occurs in brain. Cardiomyocyte death occurs in heart.  
CVD is the peripheral manifestation of central excitotoxic degeneration, unified by a shared biochemical mechanism of stress induced ferroptosis.
