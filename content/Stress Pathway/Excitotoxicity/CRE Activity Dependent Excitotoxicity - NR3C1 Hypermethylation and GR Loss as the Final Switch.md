## Overview
Chronic stress begins as an endocrine event but ends as a self-sustaining electrical process. Cortisol released from the adrenal cortex enters neurons, binds cytoplasmic glucocorticoid receptors (GRs), and the complex translocates into the nucleus to regulate genes through glucocorticoid response elements. This normally forms a feedback loop that dampens excitatory signaling and restores homeostasis once stress resolves.  

When stress is prolonged, the NR3C1 promoter becomes hypermethylated, lowering GR expression and weakening this hormonal feedback. As GRs decline, transcriptional control shifts from glucocorticoid response elements (GREs) to cAMP response elements (CREs) driven by CREB and **Nur77 (NR4A1)**. Neural activity itself begins to dictate gene expression. Calcium influx from glutamatergic signaling keeps CREB phosphorylated, maintaining transcription of NMDA and AMPA receptor subunits.  

This creates a positive feedback loop: more receptor expression increases calcium entry, activating CREB further and perpetuating receptor synthesis. Mitochondrial calcium overload generates reactive oxygen species, damaging membranes and DNA. The cell becomes locked in a state of **CRE activity dependent excitotoxicity**, where receptor upregulation is sustained by firing frequency rather than endocrine regulation [(Heling et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685035), [(Yehuda et al., 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005), [(Weaver et al., 2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1276).

## Mechanistic Sequence
1. **Cortisol entry and GR cycling** – Free cortisol diffuses into the cytoplasm, binds GR, and the complex moves to the nucleus to regulate GRE-containing genes that normally reduce excitatory tone.  
2. **Prolonged activation** – Sustained cortisol exposure traps GR in the nucleus, leading to receptor degradation and suppression of new synthesis.  
3. **Promoter hypermethylation** – Chronic stress activates DNMT1 and HDACs, hypermethylating NR3C1 and silencing GR transcription [(Yehuda et al., 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005).  
4. **Loss of cytoplasmic GR pool** – With fewer receptors, cortisol can no longer regulate transcription through GREs.  
5. **Unopposed CREB–Nur77 activation** – In the absence of GR repression, CREB and Nur77 occupy cAMP response elements (CREs) on *GRIN* and *GRIA* genes, sustaining NMDA and AMPA receptor transcription [(Heling et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685035).  
6. **Activity-dependent upregulation** – Receptor expression becomes driven by neuronal firing and intracellular Ca²⁺ flux rather than hormonal feedback. Each activation event phosphorylates CREB, perpetuating transcription.  
7. **Runaway excitotoxicity** – Excess NMDA and AMPA receptors amplify Ca²⁺ entry, mitochondrial ROS production, and oxidative damage, completing the excitotoxic cycle.

---

## Functional Interpretation
The shift from hormonal to activity-driven transcription is the final stage of the stress–excitotoxicity cascade. Once NR3C1 is hypermethylated and cytoplasmic GRs decline, the cortisol–GR feedback brake is lost. CREB and Nur77 become dominant regulators at open excitatory promoters, making NMDA and AMPA expression scale with neuronal activity rather than endocrine control. Repeated stress therefore drives receptor proliferation, Ca²⁺ overload, and mitochondrial oxidation leading to degeneration.

Conversely, hypomethylation at NR3C1 yields higher GR expression and stronger feedback. This permits rapid shutdown of CREB–Nur77 programs and reduces oxidative stress. Animal and human data indicate that demethylated NR3C1 states are protective, linking early-life environment to long-term resilience [(Weaver et al., 2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1276), [(Yehuda et al., 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005).

---

## Key Points
- GR restrains Nur77 and shortens stress-induced transcription. Heling et al. demonstrate altered Nur77 DNA-binding kinetics as the mechanism of antagonism [(Heling et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685035).  
- NR3C1 hypermethylation reduces GR expression and abolishes feedback inhibition [(Yehuda et al., 2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005).  
- CREB and Nur77 take over transcriptional control of *GRIN* and *GRIA*, maintaining NMDA and AMPA expression.  
- Receptor density becomes usage-dependent, coupling stress and neural activity to excitotoxic risk.
