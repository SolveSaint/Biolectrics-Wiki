## Overview

Plants and animals operate on the same excitation to redox architecture. Both kingdoms generate reactive oxygen species through stress and through persistent foreign object intrusion, and both produce the same ROS species through identical calcium linked biochemical logic.

Any form of stress increases intracellular calcium in both kingdoms because all stress classes converge on the same excitatory machinery. In animals, psychological, metabolic, thermal, inflammatory, and environmental stressors increase glucocorticoids, enhance glutamate receptor trafficking, raise calcium influx, and elevate mitochondrial production of superoxide and hydrogen peroxide [(Khan et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.14336/AD.2024.0125-1); [(Wu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-025-01576-w). In plants, drought, salinity, heat, cold, wounding, and pathogen pressure elevate intracellular calcium through GLR channels and activate RBOH enzymes, generating the same superoxide and hydrogen peroxide species through calcium dependent oxidation of NADPH [(Marino et al., 2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tplants.2011.10.001); [(Suzuki et al., 2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbi.2011.07.014). Stress therefore produces identical ROS species through identical biochemical logic across both kingdoms.

Persistent foreign objects create the second route, and the parallel between kingdoms is exact. In animals, macrophages surround an unremovable object such as asbestos, silica, or a persistent microbial fragment. They attempt to engulf it but cannot remove it, which creates a stable inflammatory ring of activated cells around the object. This ring produces continuous ROS and drives the kynurenine pathway toward quinolinic acid, a strong NMDA receptor agonist that amplifies calcium influx and intensifies oxidative load.

In plants, insects, nematodes, microbes, or injected effectors remain embedded in tissue and cannot be removed. The surrounding plant cells form a gall that encloses the intruder, creating a structural ring of activated tissue that directly parallels the inflammatory ring in animals. This gall tissue produces elevated auxin, which amplifies GLR mediated calcium signaling and drives RBOH dependent ROS production. Both systems therefore construct a containment structure around the foreign object, maintain chronic excitatory activation, and raise ROS through identical calcium linked pathways.

Quinolinic acid in animals and auxin in plants are ligand amplifiers that perform equivalent functions. Both raise excitatory signaling, increase calcium throughput, intensify ROS generation, and push tissue toward oxidative strain. When this excitatory load exceeds mitochondrial tolerance, both kingdoms adopt a glycolytic survival strategy. In animals this produces the early tumor microenvironment. In plants this stabilizes gall tissue. Tumors and galls are parallel hypergrowth structures formed by identical redox and ligand driven processes.

This establishes a single conserved excitatory redox mechanism across plants and animals.

## GLR and Glutamate Receptor Parallels

GLR channels in plants and NMDA and AMPA receptors in animals are functional equivalents. Both mediate calcium entry during activation and both link excitation to ROS generation. In plants, GLR channels regulate hydrogen peroxide signaling during cold and salt stress and support acclimation through calcium linked redox activity [(Li et al., 2019)](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.13623). GLR mediated nitric oxide signaling helps maintain antioxidant balance under salt stress [(Gokce et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10265-024-01528-1).

In animals, glutamate receptor activation increases calcium influx, drives mitochondrial respiration, and generates superoxide and hydrogen peroxide during excitatory states [(Khan et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.14336/AD.2024.0125-1). The mapping between GLRs and animal ionotropic glutamate receptors defines a direct structural and functional correspondence in excitatory redox coupling.

## RBOH and Mitochondrial ROS Amplification

Plants and animals use homologous machinery to amplify ROS once calcium rises.

Plants use respiratory burst oxidase homolog enzymes. These enzymes convert NADPH into superoxide and hydrogen peroxide in a calcium dependent manner and act as central ROS amplifiers during all stress states [(Marino et al., 2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tplants.2011.10.001). RBOH integrates calcium and phosphorylation inputs to regulate the magnitude of oxidative bursts [(Suzuki et al., 2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbi.2011.07.014).

Animals amplify ROS through mitochondria and NADPH oxidases. Calcium driven increases in mitochondrial electron flow generate superoxide and hydrogen peroxide through the electron transport chain, producing the same oxidants through the same biochemical sequence [(Wu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-025-01576-w).

Both kingdoms therefore generate identical oxidants through homologous calcium linked oxidase systems.

## Beneficial ROS and the Threshold to Injury

ROS regulate development, patterning, stress adaptation, and hormone signaling in plants and animals [(Noctor et al., 2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semcdb.2017.07.013). When ROS exceed antioxidant capacity the same biochemical injury occurs across both kingdoms. Excessive ROS cause lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, DNA breaks, membrane destabilization, and mitochondrial dysfunction [(Sharma et al., 2012)](https://doi.org/10.1155/2012/217037); [(Das and Roychoudhury, 2014)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenvs.2014.00053).

Any stress category that increases calcium elevates ROS in both plants and animals. In plants, drought, salinity, heat, cold, wounding, and pathogen pressure intensify ROS generation [(Hasanuzzaman et al., 2020)](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms21228695); [(Xie et al., 2019)](https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/9732325). In animals, psychological load, metabolic strain, inflammation, thermal stress, and environmental adversity all raise cortisol, enhance ionotropic glutamate signaling, and increase mitochondrial ROS generation [(Khan et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.14336/AD.2024.0125-1). These stress driven ROS increases also transmit across generations, as shown in Pekin ducks where parental stress alters offspring glucocorticoid and excitatory baseline physiology [(Oluwagbenga et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psj.2025.105838).

The ROS threshold separating adaptation from injury is therefore identical across kingdoms.

## Ligand Amplification by Auxin and Quinolinic Acid

Auxin in plants and quinolinic acid in animals amplify excitatory load in the presence of persistent foreign objects.

In animals, chronic inflammation surrounding unremovable intruders drives quinolinic acid synthesis. Quinolinic acid activates NMDA receptors, increases calcium influx, and elevates ROS generation, establishing a chronic excitatory microenvironment.

In plants, galling increases auxin concentration around the foreign presence. Auxin increases GLR mediated calcium activation and stimulates RBOH dependent ROS production, creating the same chronic excitatory state in plant tissue [(Gokce et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10265-024-01528-1).

Auxin and quinolinic acid therefore perform parallel ligand amplification roles that escalate calcium signaling, ROS output, and proliferative pressure in both kingdoms.

---

## Glycolytic Shift in Both Kingdoms

When ROS exceed mitochondrial tolerance in either kingdom, the cell shifts toward glycolysis to reduce oxidative load.

In animals, this shift defines the early tumor microenvironment and occurs in regions of chronic inflammation and excitatory stress. In plants, excessive ROS under salinity, drought, wounding, or galling suppress oxidative phosphorylation and induce glycolytic metabolism [(Hasanuzzaman et al., 2020)](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms21228695).

Galls in plants and tumors in animals represent parallel hypergrowth structures formed by identical glycolytic adaptation under persistent ligand driven activation and foreign object stress.

---

## Programmed Cell Death Parallels

When compensatory pathways fail, both kingdoms invoke ROS mediated programmed cell death. In plants, high ROS induce mitochondrial failure, chloroplast injury, cardiolipin oxidation, and cytochrome c release [(Petrov et al., 2015)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2015.00069). In animals, excitotoxic activation causes the same mitochondrial collapse and ROS triggered death pathways [(Wu et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-025-01576-w).

Programmed cell death in plants and apoptosis or degeneration in animals therefore share the same ROS linked mechanism.

---

## Unified View

Across all reviewed literature the same biological logic is present. Stress increases calcium signaling and ROS generation in plants and animals. Foreign objects produce chronic activation that cannot resolve. Auxin and quinolinic acid act as ligand amplifiers that intensify excitatory load. Both kingdoms generate superoxide and hydrogen peroxide through calcium linked oxidases and mitochondria. Both cross a redox threshold that forces a glycolytic shift to preserve survival. Both form hypergrowth structures under chronic ligand driven activation, galls in plants and tumors in animals. Both invoke ROS mediated cell death when this fails.

This establishes a single conserved excitatory redox mechanism across the plant and animal kingdoms.
