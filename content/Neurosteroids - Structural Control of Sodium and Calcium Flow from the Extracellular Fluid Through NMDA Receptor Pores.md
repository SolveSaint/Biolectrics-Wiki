### Overview
Neurosteroids reshape NMDA receptor pores, determining how easily sodium and calcium from the extracellular fluid enter the neuron during activation. Cryo-EM mapping of GluN1a-2B shows that steroid and oxysterol ligands bind to juxtamembrane pockets that physically bend the M3 helices forming the ion pathway. This bending changes pore diameter and hydration, adjusting how many ions the receptor can conduct per opening and defining the strength of each excitatory pulse [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).

### Origin and Relationship to Cortisol
Both neurosteroids and cortisol arise from cholesterol via conversion to pregnenolone within mitochondria by CYP11A1. Cortisol is produced in the adrenal cortex and acts through glucocorticoid receptors to increase NMDA and AMPA receptor expression at the genomic level. Neurosteroids are synthesized locally in brain tissue and act at the membrane surface, modulating the ion flux of existing receptors. These two routes operate in tandem: cortisol raises receptor number, while neurosteroids adjust the current each receptor conducts.

### Structural Mechanism
Neurosteroids and oxysterols bind specialized juxtamembrane pockets near the channel gate on GluN2B subunits.  
- **24S-hydroxycholesterol** stabilizes a fully open conformation where GluN1a and GluN2B helices bend outward, widening the pore and increasing sodium and calcium flow during activation [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).  
- **EU1622-240** stabilizes a sub-open state that restricts pore dilation and reduces ion flux while maintaining receptor activation [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).  
- **Pregnenolone sulfate** binds the same region in a dual-ligand configuration, producing variable pore geometries and distinct ionic conductance states [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).

### Ion Source and Flow
The ions conducted by NMDA receptors originate in the extracellular space, not within the receptor itself. When glutamate and glycine open the channel, sodium and calcium move inward along their electrochemical gradients, while potassium exits. Neurosteroid binding alters the ease of this movement by reshaping the gate region, which changes the rate of ion permeation but not ion supply. This defines how much charge and calcium signaling pressure each receptor event delivers to the neuron.

### Functional Convergence with Stress
Stress increases both cholesterol metabolism and pregnenolone synthesis. Cortisol generated through this pathway enhances receptor density via nuclear signaling. At the same time, neurosteroids reshape NMDA receptor pores so that each opening carries more sodium and calcium. This dual amplification increases total excitatory current and mitochondrial calcium uptake, driving ROS formation and excitotoxic damage within the stress–glutamate–ROS cascade.

### Summary
Neurosteroids act as structural modulators of NMDA receptor pores, controlling how strongly sodium and calcium from the extracellular fluid flow into neurons. This rapid, physical regulation complements cortisol’s genomic control of receptor expression. Together they form a unified system that links steroid metabolism, ion conductance, and oxidative stress within excitatory signaling.
