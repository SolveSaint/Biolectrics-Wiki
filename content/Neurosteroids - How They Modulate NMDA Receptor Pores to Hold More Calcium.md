### Overview
Neurosteroids alter the physical structure of NMDA receptor pores, which controls how much calcium each receptor can carry during an opening. Cryo-EM structures of GluN1a-2B show that ligands bind juxtamembrane pockets and bias the gate into fully open or sub-open conformations. The bending of M3 helices changes pore radius and hydration, which sets per-receptor calcium throughput [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).

### Origin and Relationship to Cortisol
Both neurosteroids and cortisol derive from cholesterol through pregnenolone formation in mitochondria. Cortisol forms in the adrenal cortex and acts through cytoplasmic GRs that translocate to the nucleus to regulate transcription. Neurosteroids are synthesized within brain tissue and act rapidly at membrane receptor sites. This creates a genomic path through cortisol and a membrane allosteric path through neurosteroids that converge on excitatory load.

### Structural Mechanism
Neurosteroids and oxysterols bind pockets on GluN2B near the transmembrane gate.  
- 24S-hydroxycholesterol stabilizes a fully open state where GluN1a M3 and GluN2B M3′ are bent, which increases single-channel conductance and calcium transfer per activation [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).  
- EU1622-240 stabilizes a sub-open state with partial dilation and reduced calcium permeability, which lowers calcium per activation while preserving openings [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).  
- Pregnenolone sulfate binds in the same region with a two-ligand configuration, indicating diverse steroid recognition and state dependent effects on pore geometry [(Kang et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09695-4).

### Functional Convergence with Stress
Stress elevates cholesterol mobilization and pregnenolone supply. Cortisol increases NMDA and AMPA receptor expression through nuclear signaling. Neurosteroids increase calcium carried by each receptor opening through structural bias of the pore. Together these routes increase total excitatory current, drive mitochondrial calcium overload, and raise ROS generation, which accelerates excitotoxic injury.

### Summary
Neurosteroids provide a membrane level control that determines how much calcium each NMDA receptor pore can pass per opening. This complements the endocrine control of receptor number by cortisol. The combination explains how metabolic and steroid tone regulate excitatory amplitude and oxidative burden in the stress glutamate ROS pathway.
