**Biolectrics** is a unified model explaining how **stress driven glutamate signaling** shapes bioelectric and metabolic function across generations. It describes how psychological or physiological stress increases glutamate receptor density and synaptic activity, driving calcium entry, mitochondrial overload, and the generation of reactive oxygen species (**ROS**). These processes link the nervous, endocrine, and immune systems through shared electrical and redox pathways that determine whether a cell adapts, degenerates, or transforms.  

At the genetic and epigenetic level, stress leaves a measurable imprint called the **stress methylome**. Prolonged cortisol signaling alters DNA methylation at key regulatory genes such as **NR3C1**, **FKBP5**, and **HSD11B2**, which control glucocorticoid sensitivity and feedback inhibition. These modifications are transmitted across generations, shaping baseline excitability, emotional reactivity, and metabolic set points. The stress methylome therefore preserves a biochemical memory of ancestral experience, influencing how future offspring respond to stress and environmental challenge.  

At the cellular level, chronic stress transforms the glutamate–calcium–ROS axis from an adaptive communication system into a self sustaining feedback loop. Mitochondrial calcium overload produces ROS that damage membranes and DNA, triggering **autophagy** and shifts in gene expression. As repair capacity declines, metabolism reprograms toward **glycolysis**, **glutaminolysis**, and **SLC7A11 mediated cystine uptake**, allowing survival under oxidative pressure.  

This mechanism unifies a wide range of conditions. In the nervous system it explains **neurodegeneration** and **fibromyalgia** through excitotoxic and redox injury. In the body it connects **PTSD**, **cardiometabolic disease**, and **cancer** to the same biochemical architecture of stress adaptation and mitochondrial strain. The result is a single framework linking emotion, energy, and evolution through the physics of living systems.  

**Biolectrics** maps these processes from molecular regulation to systemic outcomes and highlights where intervention can occur: restoring **glucocorticoid receptor feedback**, reducing **β adrenergic drive**, modulating **glutamate signaling**, or reinforcing antioxidant and mitochondrial repair systems.
