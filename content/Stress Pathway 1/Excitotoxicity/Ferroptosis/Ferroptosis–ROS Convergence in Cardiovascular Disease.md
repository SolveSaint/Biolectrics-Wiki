### Summary
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the global leading cause of death and expresses the same oxidative terminal mechanism observed in neurodegenerative disease. The convergence of **iron overload** and **reactive oxygen species (ROS)** results in lipid peroxidation and **ferroptotic cell death** within cardiomyocytes and vascular endothelium. This process mirrors the **stress–glutamate–ROS cascade** seen in ALS and Alzheimer’s disease, establishing a unified model of excitotoxicity-driven degeneration across the brain and heart.

---

### Mechanistic Sequence

1. **Iron Dysregulation and Fenton Chemistry**  
   Excess Fe²⁺ reacts with H₂O₂ to generate hydroxyl radicals (•OH) through the Fenton reaction, the most destructive form of ROS. These radicals attack lipids, proteins, and mitochondrial DNA, driving peroxidation and structural collapse ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)). HO-1 and TFR1 are upregulated in cardiac tissue during ischemia and heart failure, confirming chronic iron mobilization and overload ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).

2. **Mitochondrial ROS Production**  
   Mitochondria are both the source and target of oxidative damage. During ischemia and reperfusion, calcium influx overdrives oxidative phosphorylation, increasing electron leakage through complexes I and III, producing hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)). When H₂O₂ reacts with Fe²⁺, it yields hydroxyl radicals that devastate mitochondrial membranes and DNA ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)).

3. **GPX4 Depletion and GSH Loss**  
   Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) normally reduces lipid hydroperoxides, preventing membrane oxidation. Stress, hypoxia, and sustained ROS generation deplete GPX4 and glutathione (GSH), leaving polyunsaturated lipids vulnerable ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)). Ferroptotic injury is defined by this antioxidant collapse, producing uncontrolled lipid peroxidation ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).

4. **Lipid Peroxidation and Ferroptotic Rupture**  
   The oxidation of membrane phospholipids generates malondialdehyde (MDA) and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), both hallmarks of ferroptosis ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)). Elevated ACSL4 facilitates synthesis of oxidizable lipids that amplify peroxidation, culminating in outer membrane rupture and ferroptotic death ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).

5. **Inflammation and Endothelial Activation**  
   Oxidized phospholipids activate NF-κB and toll-like receptor pathways, increasing cytokine release and adhesion molecule expression ([Sugamura & Keaney, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2011.05.004)). This chronic inflammatory feedback sustains endothelial dysfunction and plaque formation, a key step in atherosclerosis ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).

6. **Ferroptotic Cell Death and Tissue Failure**  
   Iron-driven lipid peroxidation ultimately ruptures cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells, releasing DAMPs that propagate inflammatory remodeling ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)). The result is tissue necrosis, fibrotic scarring, and progressive heart failure — a process identical in molecular logic to excitotoxic neuronal death ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)).

### Parallel to Neurodegeneration

| Mechanistic Node | Cardiovascular Expression | Neural Analog |
|------------------|---------------------------|---------------|
| Iron overload (HO-1↑, TFR1↑) | Plaques, infarct rim, ischemic myocardium | ALS, AD, PD — cortical and nigral deposits |
| GPX4 depletion / GSH loss | Heart failure, I/R myocardium | Motor cortex, hippocampus |
| Lipid peroxidation (4-HNE↑) | Atherosclerotic and infarct tissue | Amyloid and Lewy body regions |
| Mitochondrial ROS | ETC leak and Ca²⁺ overload in myocardium | Complex I/III dysfunction |
| Ferroptosis inhibition (Fer-1, chelators) | Cardioprotection ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)) | Neuronal protection (same mechanism) |

### Therapeutic Insights
- **Iron chelation** limits hydroxyl radical formation and reduces reperfusion injury ([Yan et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5124553)).  
- **Ferroptosis inhibitors** such as ferrostatin-1 and liproxstatin-1 preserve myocardial tissue ([Hu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2021.710963)).  
- **NRF2 activation** restores redox control and mitigates cardiomyocyte death ([Sawicki et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.321667)).  
- **Mitochondria-targeted antioxidants** like SS-31 and MitoQ effectively lower ROS generation ([Peoples et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-019-0355-7)).  
- **Stress modulation** reduces chronic excitatory drive, preventing sustained mitochondrial overload and iron release.

---

### Interpretation
CVD and neurodegeneration lie along the same oxidative continuum. Chronic stress elevates cortisol and excitatory signaling, increasing calcium flux and mitochondrial throughput. The resulting ROS surplus, when combined with labile iron, generates hydroxyl radicals that catalyze ferroptosis. This **stress–iron–ROS triad** defines the endpoint of biological overdrive — neuronal loss in the brain and cardiomyocyte death in the heart.  
CVD is thus the **peripheral manifestation of central excitotoxic degeneration**, unified by a shared biochemical mechanism of stress-induced ferroptosis.

### Insight
The **iron–ROS–ferroptosis axis** represents the biochemical endpoint of stress and excitotoxic load. In the brain, this cascade results in neurodegeneration; in the heart, it drives ischemic and heart-failure pathology. Both arise from the same mechanism of mitochondrial overactivation, calcium overload, and iron-catalyzed oxidative collapse — the **bioelectric exhaustion state of living tissue**.
